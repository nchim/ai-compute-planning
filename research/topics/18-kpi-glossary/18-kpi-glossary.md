# KPI Glossary & Benchmarks (F1)

**Status:** 🟢 Shallow pass — 2026-09-15
**Scope:** US-primary; lens = DC developer/operator + AI-lab compute buyer

## Summary
AI data center economics metrics cluster into four layers: (1) **facility efficiency** (PUE, WUE, rack density, kW/rack) which developers/operators own and optimize; (2) **capital & financing** ($/MW capex, yield-on-cost, cap rate, interconnection lead time) which determine whether a project pencils and how fast it can be built; (3) **compute economics** (MFU, tokens/sec/GPU, cost per million tokens, GPU depreciation life) which AI labs and neoclouds use to judge whether the delivered infrastructure is actually cheap compute; and (4) **TCO allocation** (% power vs hardware vs facility) which ties the first three together into a single P&L view. The single biggest lever separating a "good" from "bad" deal in 2026 is now grid access (interconnection lead time), not construction cost — power has become the scarce input, with 5–7 year queues in PJM/ERCOT dwarfing the ~18-24mo hardware refresh cycle. GPU depreciation life is the most contested and highest-leverage assumption in the entire stack, directly swinging reported hyperscaler profitability. For compute buyers, MFU and cost-per-token are the ROI metrics; for developers, $/MW capex and yield-on-cost/cap-rate spread are the ROI metrics — but they are increasingly coupled because AI-tenant credit quality and GPU obsolescence risk feed into what cap rate a shell commands.

## Metric reference
| Metric | Definition | Typical / benchmark | Why it matters (dev vs buyer) | Source |
|--------|-----------|--------------------|------------------------------|--------|
| PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness) | Total facility power ÷ IT equipment power | Leading AI DCs ~1.1–1.2 (e.g., 1.14 at 100MW full load); older/enterprise avg often 1.5+ | Dev: core design/ops metric, drives opex and cooling capex choice (air vs liquid). Buyer: affects effective $/kWh delivered to compute | [ClearComfort PUE/WUE 2026 Benchmarks](https://clearcomfort.com/pue-wue-ai-data-centers/) |
| WUE (Water Usage Effectiveness) | Liters of water used ÷ kWh of IT energy | Not standardized; increasingly scrutinized for liquid-cooled AI halls in water-stressed regions | Dev: permitting/community risk, cooling design tradeoff vs PUE. Buyer: ESG/reporting exposure | [ClearComfort PUE/WUE 2026 Benchmarks](https://clearcomfort.com/pue-wue-ai-data-centers/) |
| $/MW capex (all-in construction) | Total construction cost per MW of capacity | Standard cloud/air-cooled: $11–14M/IT MW; AI liquid-cooled: $15–20M+/MW; all-in incl. GPUs/networking: up to ~$37M/MW by some 2026 estimates; global avg $11.3M/MW | Dev: core underwriting metric for build cost. Buyer: embedded in lease/colocation pricing they ultimately pay | [Axis Intelligence AI DC Cost per MW 2026](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-data-center-cost-per-mw/); [Archdesk AI DC Construction 2026](https://archdesk.com/blog/global-ai-data-center-construction-2026) |
| $/kW capex | Same as above, per kW (÷1000) | See $/MW above (e.g., $11-37k/kW range) | Used interchangeably with $/MW at smaller scale (colo suites, retail) | [irecruit.co DC Construction Cost per MW 2026](https://www.irecruit.co/insights/data-center-construction-cost-per-mw-2026-benchmarks-owners) |
| $/MWh or $/kW/month (power & wholesale pricing) | Price paid for power or wholesale colocation capacity | Wholesale colo: $150–220/kW/month (hyperscale, Atlanta-type market, NoVa premium above); retail colo $100–200/kW/month | Dev: revenue metric for leased capacity. Buyer: direct opex line, scales with fleet size | [datacenterHawk Colocation Pricing 2026 Guide](https://datacenterhawk.com/resources/fundamentals/colocation-data-center-pricing-a-2026-beginner-s-guide); [encoradvisors Colocation Pricing 2026](https://encoradvisors.com/data-center-colocation-pricing/) |
| Cost per token / per million tokens (inference) | $ cost to generate 1M output tokens on given hardware/model | Varies enormously by model size/architecture; SemiAnalysis publishes per-accelerator inference cost-per-million-token benchmarks across Nvidia/AMD/custom silicon | Buyer: primary unit economics metric for judging serving efficiency and margin. Dev/neocloud: sets pricing floor for GPU-as-a-service | [SemiAnalysis AI Cloud TCO Model](https://semianalysis.com/ai-cloud-tco-model/) |
| MFU (Model FLOPs Utilization) | Fraction of theoretical peak accelerator FLOPs actually used by model compute | H100 clusters historically well below peak; GB200 NVL72 shows notable MFU gains over H100 per SemiAnalysis benchmarks | Buyer: key efficiency metric — low MFU means paying for idle silicon. Dev: less direct, but informs which hardware/generation to plan power+cooling around | [SemiAnalysis H100 vs GB200 NVL72 Training Benchmarks](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/h100-vs-gb200-nvl72-training-benchmarks); [Medium: Using MFU](https://medium.com/better-ml/using-model-flops-utilization-mfu-7b17de07faec) |
| Tokens/sec/GPU | Inference throughput per accelerator | Highly model/hardware dependent; central metric in SemiAnalysis InferenceMAX-style benchmarking | Buyer: direct driver of $/token; used to compare GPU generations/vendors | [SemiAnalysis AI Cloud TCO Model](https://semianalysis.com/ai-cloud-tco-model/) |
| Power density (kW/rack) | Power draw per rack | GB200 NVL72 ~120–140kW/rack; GB300 NVL72 ~132–142kW/rack; Vera Rubin (VR200) NVL72 ~190–230kW (H2 2026); Kyber NVL576 ~600kW (H2 2027 target); typical enterprise modal density only ~11kW (2026, up from 9kW in 2025) | Dev: drives cooling architecture choice (air vs direct-liquid) and shell design. Buyer: determines cluster footprint and interconnect topology | [mike bommarito: Rack Density Evolution](https://michaelbommarito.com/wiki/datacenters/technology/rack-density/); [rax.ae GB200 NVL72 Guide 2026](https://rax.ae/knowledge-center/articles/nvidia-blackwell-gb200-nvl72-data-center-guide.html) |
| Rack/cluster size | GPUs per rack / racks per cluster | NVL72 = 72 GPUs/rack; large training clusters now scale to tens of thousands of GPUs across many racks | Dev: physical/electrical planning unit. Buyer: determines max model training scale achievable per site | [GB200 Hardware Architecture — SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/gb200-hardware-architecture-and-component) |
| Critical IT load (MW) | Power capacity actually available to IT equipment (excludes overhead/redundancy losses) | Hyperscale campuses now routinely 100MW–1GW+ in planning; site-level building blocks often 30–100MW halls | Dev: the number that drives interconnection application size and phasing. Buyer: caps how much compute can be deployed at a given site | [Archdesk AI DC Construction 2026](https://archdesk.com/blog/global-ai-data-center-construction-2026) |
| Yield-on-cost (development) | Stabilized NOI ÷ total development cost | 7.00–8.50% typical for AI/hyperscale development in 2026 | Dev: core underwriting return metric, compared against exit cap rate to justify development risk | [MaxLife Data Center Cap Rates 2026](https://maxlifedevelopment.com/blog/data-center-cap-rates-2026) |
| Stabilized cap rate | Exit/market cap rate on a leased, operating asset | Hyperscale NNN: 5.0–6.5% (JLL Q1 2026); colocation 6–7%; powered shell 7–8.5% | Dev: sets valuation ceiling/floor and financing terms; spread vs yield-on-cost = development profit margin | [MaxLife Data Center Cap Rates 2026](https://maxlifedevelopment.com/blog/data-center-cap-rates-2026) |
| GPU depreciation life | Useful economic life assumption used for accounting depreciation | Hyperscalers currently use 5–6 years (some extended from 3-4yrs); critics (e.g., Michael Burry) argue true economic life is 2–3 years given 18–24mo Nvidia architecture cadence; industry may converge ~5yrs | Buyer/hyperscaler: directly swings reported net income/margins — a top 2026 accounting controversy. Dev/neocloud: affects lease structuring and refresh capex planning | [CNBC: How long before a GPU depreciates?](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/14/ai-gpu-depreciation-coreweave-nvidia-michael-burry.html); [Dave Friedman: The $176B Accounting Question](https://davefriedman.substack.com/p/the-176-billion-accounting-question) |
| Interconnection lead time | Time from grid interconnection application to power delivery | PJM: large AI campuses in Northern Virginia approaching ~7 years; ERCOT: gas ~17mo, renewables ~27mo, storage ~23mo for interconnection agreement (though large-load queue is separate and much longer); US median across all projects approaching ~5 years; power transformers ~128 weeks lead time | Dev: now the single biggest schedule risk/bottleneck, often exceeding construction time itself. Buyer: determines when contracted capacity actually goes live | [EnkiAI: US Grid Congestion 2026 PJM](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/us-grid-congestion-2026-pjms-data-center-crisis/); [Ascend Analytics: Interconnection Queues](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/large-load-interconnection-queues-data-center-grid-access) |
| TCO breakdown (power vs hardware vs facility) | Allocation of total cost across opex/capex categories | Opex: power ~60-70% of operating cost. Capex (traditional DC): power+cooling ~60-70% of construction hard cost (land/shell ~20%, mechanical ~20%, power infra ~20%, IT equipment ~40%). For AI halls specifically: hardware ($500M-$1B in a 20MW hall) now exceeds building cost ($200-300M) | Dev: informs where to focus cost engineering (power infra vs shell). Buyer: hardware now dominates total AI infrastructure spend, more than the building itself | [globaldatacenterhub: What a Data Center Actually Costs](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/what-a-data-center-actually-costs); [Thunder Said Energy: Economic costs of data-centers](https://thundersaidenergy.com/downloads/data-centers-the-economics/) |
| Vacancy rate (colocation) | % of built capacity unleased | Record low 1.4% in primary North American colo markets, year-end 2025 | Dev: signals pricing power / undersupply. Buyer: signals scarcity/lead time risk for procuring capacity | [datacenterHawk Colocation Pricing 2026 Guide](https://datacenterhawk.com/resources/fundamentals/colocation-data-center-pricing-a-2026-beginner-s-guide) |
