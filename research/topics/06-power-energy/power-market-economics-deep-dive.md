# Power Market Economics Deep Dive: AI Data Centers (US, as of Sept 2026)

Scope: US-primary, PJM/Northern Virginia and ERCOT/Texas emphasized. All $/MWh figures are nominal 2026 unless noted. Sources are free/public (EIA, ISO filings, law-firm client alerts summarizing regulatory dockets, trade press, analyst blogs). Purchased reports (Wood Mackenzie, S&P full reports, LevelTen full datasets) are cited only where a free summary/press release disclosed the number.

## 2. Behind-the-meter gas: the quantified time-value case

**The core trade:** pay roughly $50–100/MWh more than a grid-firmed alternative to compress the delivery timeline from 5–7 years (PJM queue) or growing ERCOT queue times to 18–30 months.

- BTM all-in cost: **$100–165/MWh** (RaboResearch), vs. grid alternative **$90–95/MWh** cited in the same analysis — a premium in the **$10–75/MWh** range depending on overbuild/bridge assumptions. [Rabobank](https://www.rabobank.com/knowledge/d011532274-the-marathon-can-behind-the-meter-data-center-power-solutions-endure-the-decade-in-the-us)
- Timeline compression: BTM gas buildable in **18–30 months** vs. PJM interconnection now averaging **~7 years** for large Northern Virginia loads, and a national queue backlog of **~2,600 GW** with an **~80% withdrawal rate** (established context figures). ERCOT is nominally 3–4 years but its queue has exploded (see §3), pushing effective wait times up.
- Time-value math: for a hyperscaler racing a multi-billion-dollar GPU cluster, 4–5 years of accelerated revenue/compute availability dwarfs a $10–75/MWh power-cost premium. At even a modest 300 MW campus running near-continuously, that premium is on the order of $20–100M/year in extra power spend — trivial against the value of not stranding $5–15B of GPU capex waiting for grid power.
- Speed is also why BTM gas is being paired with **grid interconnection as a parallel, slower path**: many developers run BTM turbines as a "bridge" for years 1–5 while their PJM/ERCOT grid interconnection application works through the queue, then either keep the BTM plant as backup/peaking or sell excess capacity back.
- Tradeoffs: permitting (state air-quality permits, sometimes contested locally), emissions (new gas capacity undercuts corporate net-zero pledges — several hyperscalers have had to caveat 24/7-carbon-free targets), gas supply/pipeline capacity constraints in some regions (e.g., Permian associated gas vs. new pipeline buildout in Texas), and the turbine backlog itself: GE Vernova's backlog is now **116 GW** with deliveries slipping to 2031, Siemens Energy **69 GW**, Mitsubishi **35 GW**; lead times for large-frame turbines are commonly quoted at **3–5 years, and up to 7–8 years** for some frames — meaning even BTM gas is no longer the "fast" escape valve it was in 2023–24 unless a developer already has turbines on order. [Turbomachinery Magazine](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/ge-vernova-gas-turbine-backlog-hits-116-gw-as-power-orders-more-than-double); [GasTurbineHub](https://gasturbinehub.com/the-growing-backlog-of-gas-turbine-orders-implications-for-customers/)

## 3. Which markets are driving the 30–50% 2026→2028 slippage

Consensus estimate: **30–50% of large-scale data center capacity slated for 2026 will likely slip**, per Currence/Sightline Climate research reported across multiple outlets (though SemiAnalysis has pushed back on the more alarmist "half of US data centers canceled" framing — the underlying driver is real but the headline number is contested). Of ~16 GW scheduled for 2026 commissioning, only ~5 GW was under active construction as of the analysis, leaving ~11 GW in "announced" status against a typical 12–18 month build cycle. [Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4201941/up-to-50-of-data-center-capacity-slated-for-2026-could-be-delayed.html); [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/up-to-half-of-the-worlds-data-centers-may-be-delayed-this-year/); [SemiAnalysis pushback](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/stop-saying-half-of-2026-us-datacenter)

**Where the committed GW sits vs. where the queue is worst:**

- **PJM / Northern Virginia (the largest concentration of committed load):** PJM's reformed Cycle 1 interconnection queue (results released May 2026) drew **811 projects requesting 220 GW** — more than PJM's entire installed capacity. PJM is projecting a **6.6 GW capacity deficit for 2027–2028**, and system-wide demand growth of **30+ GW by 2030**, driven largely by data centers. Loudoun County/Northern Virginia — the single densest data-center cluster on Earth — is the epicenter of the ~7-year interconnection wait. This is the single biggest source of slippage risk because it combines the largest absolute committed load with the slowest queue. [power-eng.com](https://www.power-eng.com/business/at-long-last-the-pjm-interconnection-queue-is-moving-again-now-what/); [EnkiAI/PJM](https://enkiai.com/data-center/pjm-grid-constraints-data-centers/)
- **ERCOT / Texas:** nominally the "fast" market (3–4 year interconnection vs. PJM's 7), but the large-load queue has gone parabolic — from **63 GW at end-2024** to **226 GW by November 2025** to a reported **410 GW / 198 GW of Q1-2026 applications alone**, with data centers making up **~77–90%** of that load. ERCOT's total requests (~474 GW) are now more than 5x system peak demand. This explosive growth is precisely what triggered the state-ordered audit (§4), which itself is now a source of 2026→2028 slippage for Texas projects that were counting on ERCOT's speed advantage. [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/ercots-large-load-queue-has-nearly-quadrupled-in-a-single-year/); [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/14/texas-data-center-approval-pause-ercot-power-grid/)
- **Other named bottlenecks:** Phoenix/Arizona (power shortages), Santa Clara/Silicon Valley (local transmission limits), and broadly national **transformer/switchgear/battery-backup backorders of 18–36 months**, which delay projects even where grid interconnection itself is not the constraint. [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/up-to-half-of-the-worlds-data-centers-may-be-delayed-this-year/)

**Bottom line:** PJM/Virginia is the biggest single driver of slippage by absolute GW (largest committed pipeline sitting behind the slowest queue); ERCOT/Texas is the fastest-growing risk because its queue growth rate outpaced even PJM's and the state has now self-imposed a multi-month regulatory pause on top of interconnection physics.

## 4. Texas grid-connection audit (Aug 2026) — what it is and its effect on ERCOT's "bring-your-own-power" edge

- **Trigger:** On **August 3, 2026**, Governor Greg Abbott directed the PUC of Texas and ERCOT to conduct a "comprehensive verification and audit" of all data centers moving through ERCOT's interconnection process before any additional ones can proceed — triggered by the queue's explosive growth (see §3: ~474 GW of requests, ~90% data centers, >5x system peak demand) and concerns about speculative/duplicate interconnection requests inflating the queue. [Texas Governor's office](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-directs-comprehensive-data-center-audit); [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/03/texas-data-center-project-audit-greg-abbott/)
- **Scope:** ERCOT paused its "Batch Zero" interconnection study process — it announced on Aug 3 it would miss the Aug 7, 2026 Batch Zero deadline. **362 large energy consumers** had already been approved for inclusion in that study. The audit itself will cover roughly **250–300 (some reports: up to 461) proposed projects**, mostly data centers, checking each for compliance with PUCT/ERCOT/state-law requirements; any project found in violation "must be denied connection to the Texas grid." As of Sept 11–14, 2026, ERCOT is running a ~130-question survey of **461 data centers'** water and energy usage, due back mid-October 2026. [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/texas-pauses-data-center-interconnections-pending-statewide-audit); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/texas-gov-abbott-directs-data-center-audit); [Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/energy-environment/2026/09/11/561649/ercot-to-survey-more-400-data-centers-as-part-of-gov-abbott-audit/)
- **Timeline:** ERCOT is targeting a **December 10, 2026** filing to the PUCT with a comprehensive report and a project-by-project "report card"; results are expected to be presented to the governor, PUCT, and legislature in December 2026. [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ercot-texas-puc-data-center-audit/828472/); [Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/central-austin/government/ercot-to-audit-nearly-300-proposed-data-centers-by-the-end-of-2026-officials-say/)
- **Effect on ERCOT's speed advantage:** This directly erodes ERCOT/Texas's historic differentiator (3–4 yr interconnection vs. PJM's 7). Every project caught in Batch Zero is now frozen for a minimum of ~4-5 months (Aug→Dec 2026) before it can even resume queue processing, and projects found deficient face outright denial. Combined with SB6 / PUCT draft rule 16 TAC 25.194 (financial-security deposits of **$50,000/MW**, site-control verification, and mandatory curtailment exposure for loads ≥75 MW), Texas is converting from "come as you are, connect fast" to a gatekept, collateral-heavy process much closer in spirit (if not yet in years) to PJM's. This is a direct contributor to the 2026→2028 slippage: Texas capacity that developers counted on as the "fast lane" now has an indeterminate multi-month-to-multi-year regulatory overhang layered on top of physical interconnection timelines. [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/what-governor-abbotts-data-center-audit-directive-means-for-ercot-and-the-batch-zero-study-process/); [Ascend Analytics](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/large-load-interconnection-queues-data-center-grid-access)

## 5. Nuclear PPA reality check: fast vs. slow, and realistic delivered-GW timeline

Of the **~9.8 GW across 13 disclosed deals** (established baseline), the split is heavily weighted toward slow, speculative capacity:

**"Fast" bucket — existing-plant restarts/uprates (near-term, de-risked):**
- **Microsoft/Constellation — Three Mile Island Unit 1 "Crane Clean Energy Center," 835 MW**, ~$1.6B restart capex (~$1,916/kW), 20-year PPA reportedly ~$110–115/MWh, target restart **2027**. This is the only major deal with a credible near-term (sub-2-year-from-now) delivery date because it reuses an existing licensed site and turbine hall. [ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/59329-three-mile-island-unit-eyes-16b-revamp-restart-in-microsoft-deal)
- **Palisades restart (Holtec, $1.5B DOE loan, 805 MW)** — not a hyperscaler-direct PPA but part of the same "reopen shuttered plants" wave; similar 2–3 year restart timeline. [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/nuclear/palisades-three-mile-island-nuclear-restart)
- **Amazon/Talen — Susquehanna** uprate/co-location deal: priced close to wholesale market with IRA §45U credit support; effectively immediate since the plant is already operating — the "fastest" nuclear power on the list because no new construction or restart is required at all.
- Realistically, this fast bucket totals roughly **1.5–2.5 GW** of the 9.8 GW headline, deliverable **2026–2028**.

**"Slow" bucket — new SMR construction (speculative, back-half-of-decade-or-later):**
- **Google/Kairos Power** (500 MW committed via Kairos KP-FHR), **Amazon/X-energy** ($700M investment, up to 12 Xe-100 units), **Meta's up to 6.6 GW** spread across TerraPower Natrium, Oklo Aurora, Vistra, and Constellation — the large majority of the 9.8 GW headline is SMR/new-build commitments, not operating capacity.
- First Western SMR unit (GEH BWRX-300 at Darlington, Canada — not even a US/data-center-dedicated unit) targeted for **2029**; US data-center-dedicated SMR deliveries realistically stretch **2030–2035**, and that's before accounting for FOAK cost overruns (FOAK LCOE $80–150/MWh vs. unproven NOAK targets of $60–80/MWh) and licensing risk — no US SMR has yet completed NRC licensing and construction on the timeline vendors are quoting.
- This slow bucket is **roughly 7–8 GW** of the 9.8 GW headline, with **little to no realistic delivery before 2030**, and material risk (cost overrun, cancellation — cf. NuScale's earlier project cancellation) that a large fraction never delivers on the announced schedule at all.

**Bottom line on delivered-GW timeline:** treat the ~9.8 GW figure as a *commitment* number, not a *delivery-by-2028* number. Realistic 2026–2028 nuclear delivery to data centers is on the order of **1.5–2.5 GW** (restarts/uprates); the remaining **~7 GW+** is a 2030s bet contingent on SMR vendors hitting cost and schedule targets that no one has yet demonstrated at scale. [axis-intelligence.com](https://axis-intelligence.com/nuclear-energy-for-data-centers/); [smrintel.com tracker](https://smrintel.com/nuclear-data-center-deals/); [enkiai.com](https://enkiai.com/data-center/microsoft-nuclear-x-energy/)
