**Why the middle is thin and the ends are fat:** Chips (NVIDIA), the foundry/packaging chokepoint (TSMC CoWoS, fully booked through 2026), and memory (SK hynix HBM, >40% of revenue at ~80%+ margin) all sit at genuine, temporarily monopolistic supply bottlenecks — CoWoS capacity and HBM output cannot scale as fast as demand, so pricing power stays with the scarce input. Server OEMs (Dell, Supermicro) are pure assemblers/integrators competing on thin, commoditized margins (mid-single-digit on AI systems) because the GPU/HBM bill-of-materials dominates cost and OEMs add little differentiated value. Neoclouds/colo operators bear the capital-intensity and obsolescence risk (4-yr depreciation schedules against multi-year leases, uncertain re-sale value of prior-gen GPUs) while competing on undifferentiated compute — a classic capital-intensive commodity middle, further squeezed by NVIDIA's revenue-share/financing terms. At the top of the stack, AI labs with real product-market fit (Anthropic's API business, in particular) recapture margin because token costs are falling faster than what they can charge, and software/app-layer economics revert to normal SaaS-like gross margins once compute costs are amortized against usage. The stack rewards owners of scarce physical inputs (chips, packaging, memory) and owners of differentiated software/demand (frontier labs with pricing power) — and squeezes everyone renting undifferentiated capacity in between.

## 2. Circular Financing Map

Structure (as reported, 2026): NVIDIA invests equity/credit into AI labs and neoclouds (e.g., OpenAI, CoreWeave) → those labs/neoclouds spend the cash buying NVIDIA chips and/or cloud capacity from hyperscalers (Microsoft, Oracle) → hyperscalers use that revenue to buy more NVIDIA chips and build data centers → NVIDIA books it all as data-center revenue growth, which supports its stock price and enables further investment. Concretely reported flow: "Nvidia plans to invest in OpenAI, which is buying cloud computing from Oracle, which is buying chips from Nvidia, which has a stake in CoreWeave, which is providing AI infrastructure to OpenAI." Separately, "OpenAI now uses Microsoft's money to pay Microsoft, which in turn pays Oracle to provide the infrastructure, which is filled with Nvidia chips."

- **Scale:** 2026 analyses estimate **>$800B** in circular/vendor-financed arrangements across the ecosystem. ([Bloomberg — AI circular deals](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-circular-deals/); [Curionic — circular financing web](https://www.curionic.net/2026/08/ai-circular-financing-nvidia-openai-oracle-coreweave-2026.html))
- **A specific deflation data point:** the reported OpenAI–Nvidia data-center compute deal came in **$145B lower than initially reported**, which some read as a signal that headline circular-deal totals were partly aspirational/PR rather than contracted. ([Fortune — OpenAI-Nvidia deal](https://fortune.com/2026/08/18/openai-data-center-deal-with-nvidia-comes-in-145-billion-lower-than-reportedsignaling-concerns-of-artificial-demand-for-chips/))
- **Market's own risk-pricing of this:** CoreWeave's stock fell from a $187 peak to $89.93 (>50%) amid investor concern over both a broader AI demand slowdown and the specific circularity of its NVIDIA relationship. ([Bloomberg — circular deals](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-circular-deals/))
- **Why this is non-diversified, correlated risk:** cash "leaves a chipmaker's balance sheet as an investment, and returns to its income statement as revenue" after passing through one or two intermediaries — meaning the same dollar of NVIDIA capital gets counted (in market narrative, and partly in accounting) as demand validation multiple times. If end-demand (inference usage, enterprise AI adoption) disappoints, the shock propagates through every node simultaneously: NVIDIA's revenue, the labs' compute costs/runway, the neoclouds' debt service, and the hyperscalers' capex ROI all reference the *same* underlying demand assumption. There is no independent second signal — chip demand, cloud demand, and lab revenue are the same bet worn three times, financed increasingly with debt (vendor financing, GPU-backed loans, and neocloud bonds) rather than equity, which raises the severity of a downturn (leverage does not distribute losses, it concentrates them on whoever holds the debt).

## 3. Overbuild / Utilization Scenario Analysis

**Capacity pipeline vs. build vs. utilization (established + new data):**
- Global operational data center capacity, year-end 2025: ~62 GW.
- Active AI-dedicated/AI-substantial development pipeline, Q2 2026: **458 facilities, 85.4 GW planned IT load** — i.e., the pipeline alone would nearly **triple** total existing global capacity if fully realized. ([BloombergNEF / ClusterBid pipeline data](https://clusterbid.com/blog/ai-data-center-construction-pipeline-2026-2028-planned-capacity-region))
- US 2026 specifically: ~16 GW announced pipeline for the year vs. only ~5 GW actually under active construction — a **~7 GW / ~44%** gap between announcement and ground-truth build. ([tech-insider.org — 7GW capacity crisis](https://tech-insider.org/us-ai-data-center-delays-cancellations-7gw-capacity-crisis-2026/))
- 30–50% of US 2026 projects are expected to be delayed or cancelled (consistent with the prior "30–50% of 2026 capacity may slip to 2028" fact). ([mgrid — grid delays 50% of 2026 AI capacity at risk](https://mgrid.org/2026/01/15/data-center-grid-delays-50-percent-2026-ai-capacity-risk/))
- Current assumed US data-center utilization: **~70%**. ([tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/us-ai-data-center-delays-cancellations-7gw-capacity-crisis-2026/))
- The binding constraint has shifted from chips/capital to **power infrastructure** (grid interconnect queues, transformers, switchgear) — meaning overbuild risk today is throttled less by financial discipline than by physical/regulatory bottlenecks, which is a double-edged sword: it caps downside (can't build faster than power allows) but also means committed capital can sit stranded waiting on power for years.
- **GPU-cluster-level breakeven:** a debt-financed cluster needs **~70–75% utilization** to break even; below that, deep losses (-$80–130k/month per 1,024-GPU H100 cluster at 70%); above it, strong profit (+$170–220k/month at 90%). ([Hashrate Index](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/neocloud-unit-economics-miners-gpu-only-clouds/))

**What utilization is "needed" to justify capex, synthesized:** at the facility/cluster level, ~70–75% sustained utilization is the rough line between loss and profit under current debt-financed cost structures and 4-year depreciation assumptions; the *system* is currently operating close to that line (~70% assumed US utilization) with very little margin for a demand air-pocket, and roughly a third to half of announced future capacity is not going to show up on schedule to test it.

**Bull case:** Jevons paradox — as inference gets cheaper, usage grows faster than the cost decline, so aggregate compute demand rises even as unit economics fall; evidence cited includes agentic/coding workloads going from ~11% to >50% of token volume on OpenRouter between early 2025 and March 2026, and "structural Jevons paradox" models where falling prices induce deeper reasoning loops and multi-agent workflows that consume more compute per task, not less. Number: token volume mix shift (coding ~11%→>50% of platform tokens) as direct evidence of induced demand. ([SGNL / Jevons Paradox in AI](https://sgnl.blog/2026-03-28-jevons-paradox-inference/); [NavoKit — Jevons Paradox](https://www.navokit.com/en/blog/jevons-paradox-ai-compute))

**Bear case:** the fiber/telecom 1990s analogy — an industry built ahead of demand on the (wrong) assumption that traffic would double every ~100 days, leading to a capacity glut and a wave of bankruptcies even though the long-run need for the capacity was directionally correct; today's AI buildout risks the same "right direction, wrong speed" error, compounded by circular financing that inflates apparent demand signals. Number: the pipeline-to-installed-base ratio (85.4 GW planned vs. 62 GW installed, i.e., ~138% of existing global capacity in the pipeline) mirrors the scale of the telecom overbuild's demand/supply mismatch. ([IEEE ComSoc — fiber vs AI data centers](https://techblog.comsoc.org/2025/09/27/big-tech-spending-on-ai-data-centers-and-infrastructure-vs-the-fiber-optic-buildout-during-the-dot-com-boom-bust/); [Opus Interactive — telecom overbuild](https://www.opusinteractive.com/opus-blog/post/the-telecom-overbuild-that-built-the-cloud-lessons-from-fiber-glut-to-hyperscale-success/))

**Where the telecom analogy holds vs. breaks:**
- *Holds:* massive forward-looking capex justified by extrapolated demand curves; heavy reliance on vendor/debt financing; circular deals between suppliers and customers (Nortel/Lucent vendor financing ≈ NVIDIA vendor financing today); a real underlying long-term need that was directionally correct but wildly mistimed.
- *Breaks:* (1) fiber is a nearly non-depreciating, multi-decade asset once laid (stranded fiber from 2001 became valuable dark fiber a decade later) — GPUs depreciate fast (3–4 yr useful life is itself contested/understated per the Burry critique) and become obsolete, so "buy it and wait for demand to catch up" is a much worse strategy for compute than it was for fiber; (2) AI labs today have real, fast-growing revenue (Anthropic ARR ~$1B→$47B in 18 months) unlike most dot-com-era companies, so the demand-generation side of the bear case is weaker than in 2000; (3) the binding constraint (power/grid) throttles the *pace* of overbuild in a way fiber-laying was not throttled, which could either soften a glut (natural pacing) or worsen the mismatch (stranded partial builds waiting on interconnects).

## 4. Decision Framework — Value Capture & Overbuild Exposure

For a developer or buyer deciding where and how to commit capital/compute spend, the key variables to reason through explicitly:

1. **Position on the margin stack** — are you exposed to a scarcity chokepoint (chips, CoWoS packaging, HBM) with pricing power, or to a commoditized, capital-intensive middle layer (colo, neocloud, server OEM) that captures spread only via utilization and contract terms? Thin-margin middle positions need explicit utilization and re-contracting protection; fat-margin positions need to ask how durable the scarcity is (packaging/HBM capacity is being built out fast — margins likely compress as CoWoS scales past 120k wpm).
2. **Utilization sensitivity** — what's the breakeven utilization rate given the actual financing structure (debt-financed clusters ~70–75% breakeven vs. equity-financed which tolerates lower utilization but with lower ROE)? Model the P&L swing across a realistic utilization band (e.g., 55–90%) rather than a single point estimate.
3. **Depreciation/useful-life assumption** — GPU useful life (3, 4, 6 years?) drives reported profitability more than almost any other lever; test sensitivity to the Burry-style critique that useful lives may be understated by ~$176B industry-wide.
4. **Price decay path for the input you sell** — inference/token prices are falling 5–10x from 2024 levels; a developer/operator needs a view on whether *volume* growth (Jevons) outpaces *price* decline for their specific customer segment, not the market in aggregate.
5. **Exposure to circular/vendor financing** — how much of your revenue or your customer's revenue traces back to the same small set of circularly-financed counterparties (NVIDIA, OpenAI, Microsoft, Oracle, CoreWeave)? A concentration map of counterparty circularity is a genuine risk metric, not just a narrative concern.
6. **Physical bottleneck timing** — power/grid interconnect queues are now the pacing constraint; a project's real risk is often "will this even get built/energized on schedule" rather than "will demand be there," which changes how much overbuild risk actually is realized (delay caps the downside but also delays revenue).
7. **Demand growth vs. capacity pipeline ratio at the relevant geography** — compare local/regional committed pipeline to realistic utilized demand, not national aggregates; overbuild risk is highly regional (grid-constrained metros vs. power-abundant ones).

### What to expose as tool levers

- **Utilization slider** (e.g., 50–95%) driving cluster/facility-level P&L, with a visible breakeven line (~70–75% under debt financing).
- **Depreciation useful-life slider** (2–8 years) showing effect on reported EBIT/net income for the neocloud/operator layer.
- **Price-decay-per-year slider** for compute/inference ($/token or $/GPU-hour) paired with a **demand-elasticity slider** (Jevons multiplier) so users can see net revenue effect of "price falls X%, volume rises Y%."
- **Margin-stack waterfall** showing where a dollar of end-customer spend allocates across chip/foundry/packaging/memory/OEM/colo/neocloud/lab layers, adjustable to reflect a specific deal structure.
- **Circular-financing exposure meter** — % of a given counterparty's revenue/financing traceable to a small set of circularly-linked parties (NVIDIA/OpenAI/Microsoft/Oracle/CoreWeave-style loop), as a concentration-risk flag.
- **Pipeline-vs-installed ratio and delay-probability toggle** by region, letting users apply the 30–50% delay/cancellation base rate to committed GW and see revised utilized-capacity timelines.
- **Bull/base/bear scenario presets** pre-loading the Jevons (bull), historical-base-rate (base), and telecom-overbuild-analogy (bear) parameter sets so users can see the P&L/valuation spread across scenarios without manually tuning every slider.

## Key unresolved question

There is no reliable, independent measure of *actual utilized* AI compute (as opposed to installed/contracted capacity) at the system level — nearly every public data point is either committed/announced capacity (85.4 GW pipeline), under-construction capacity (5 GW of 16 GW US 2026 pipeline), or firm-specific utilization claims (70% US assumption, 70-75% neocloud breakeven) that are not aggregated or audited across the industry. Because so much of the demand signal itself flows through circularly-financed counterparties (NVIDIA→labs→clouds→NVIDIA), it is not yet possible to say with confidence whether the ~70% utilization figures reflect genuine end-customer demand or partly reflect capacity being "warehoused" under long-term commitments between related parties. Resolving this — i.e., getting a trustworthy, third-party utilization/occupancy metric decoupled from circular counterparties — is the single most important open data gap for judging whether the system is in bull (Jevons-driven, self-sustaining) or bear (telecom-style, circularly-inflated) territory.
