# Demand Forecasting, Overbuild & Bubble Risk (15)

**Status:** 🟢 Shallow pass — 2026-09-15
**Scope:** US-primary; lens = DC developer/operator + AI-lab compute buyer

## Summary
Major forecasters converge on massive AI-driven data center growth through 2030 — McKinsey projects global capacity nearly tripling to 219 GW (roughly 70% AI-driven, ~$5.2T AI-related capex vs. $1.5T for traditional IT), while Goldman Sachs projects global data center power demand up 165-170% by 2030 vs. 2023/2025 baselines. Hyperscaler AI capex is estimated to grow from ~$410B (2025) to $700B+ (2026), with FY2027 spending estimates for the 14 largest public data center developers up 56% between August 2025 and February 2026 alone — a sign that forecasts themselves are still being revised sharply upward. Three distinct bubble-risk vectors have emerged in 2026 discourse: (1) NVIDIA's "circular financing" web, in which it simultaneously acts as chip vendor, equity investor, and credit guarantor to the same labs/clouds buying its chips, obscuring how much demand is organic; (2) GPU/server depreciation schedules that hyperscalers have stretched from ~3-4 years to 5-6 years (Meta as far as 11-12 years), which Michael Burry estimates understates hyperscaler depreciation by ~$176B cumulative 2026-2028, given 2-3 year real obsolescence cycles (Hopper 2022 → Blackwell 2024 → Rubin 2026 → Rubin Ultra 2027); and (3) execution/timeline risk, where an estimated 30-50% of planned 2026 AI data center capacity may slip to 2028 due to power-grid interconnection queues and construction bottlenecks — even as North American hyperscaler self-build capex forecasts have moved only ~1%, suggesting near-term capital commitment is structurally sticky regardless of demand uncertainty.

## Key findings
- McKinsey: global data center capacity to nearly triple to 219 GW by 2030 (156 GW AI-related, ~70% of new demand); AI-equipped data centers require ~$5.2T capex vs. $1.5T for traditional IT data centers through 2030 ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ai-could-drive-67-trillion-investment-in-data-centers-maybe-claims-mckinsey/); [Axis Intelligence](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-data-center-forecast-2030/))
- Goldman Sachs: global data center power demand +165% by 2030 (vs. 2023) / some updates cite +170% by 2030 vs. 2025; US power demand to double by 2027 ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/ai-to-drive-165-increase-in-data-center-power-demand-by-2030); [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/us-data-center-power-demand-projected-to-double-by-2027))
- Hyperscaler AI capex: ~$410B (2025) → $700B+ (2026) estimated; FY2027 spending estimates for the top 14 public DC developers rose 56% between August 2025 and February 2026 ([SemiAnalysis-adjacent aggregation, via search synthesis](https://semianalysis.com/datacenter-industry-model/))
- >23 GW of data center capacity was under construction globally as of end-September 2025 ([BloombergNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/data-centers/ai-data-center-build-advances-at-full-speed-five-things-to-know/))
- Execution risk: an estimated 30-50% of planned 2026 AI data center capacity may slip to 2028 due to power-grid interconnection queues and construction bottlenecks; yet North American hyperscaler self-build capex forecasts have moved only ~1% despite headline cancellation stories — suggesting capital commitment is "stickier" than physical delivery timelines
- NVIDIA circular financing: up to $100B invested directly in OpenAI; $500B in financing partnerships overall (vendor financing, revenue-sharing compute deals, GPU-as-collateral lending, DGX Cloud/CoreWeave/Lambda leasing structures); Huang cites ~$100B of contracted customer demand behind financed projects as rebuttal ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jimosman/2026/08/16/nvidia-ai-financing-is-the-500-billion-risk-investors-arent-watching/); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/prediction-markets/26/09/61740701/nvidia-circular-financing-jensen-huang))
- Anthropic's $35B Lambda deal illustrates deep circularity: NVIDIA funds the lab, funds the cloud (Lambda), sells the cloud its chips, rents back ~18,000 GPUs, and reportedly holds the lease on the underlying Texas site ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jimosman/2026/08/16/nvidia-ai-financing-is-the-500-billion-risk-investors-arent-watching/))
- Depreciation: hyperscalers extended AI server/GPU useful life from ~3-4 years to 5-6 years (Meta to 5.5 years, cutting 2025 depreciation expense by $2.9B; AWS stays closer to 4 years; Meta has pushed some assets to 11-12 years); Michael Burry estimates ~$176B of understated depreciation across major hyperscalers 2026-2028, projecting Oracle's earnings overstated 26.9% and Meta's 20.8% by 2028 ([HBR case](https://store.hbr.org/product/meta-accounting-for-ai-data-center-depreciation/126034); [Natural Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/deep-quarry-useful-lives-gpus-key-considerations); search synthesis on Burry estimate)
- Real chip-generation obsolescence cadence is now annual: Hopper (2022) → Blackwell (2024) → Rubin (2026) → Rubin Ultra (2027), implying genuine 2-3 year frontier obsolescence that conflicts with 5-6+ year depreciation schedules
- Meta capex trajectory: $72.2B (2025) → ~$135B projected (2026), largely data centers and GPUs — illustrative of the scale of the bet being depreciated over extended schedules

## Insights & implications (developers / compute buyers)
- Developers should treat "committed backlog" and "contracted demand" figures (CoreWeave's $99.4B, Stargate's $400B+) as encompassing a wide range of binding-ness — some is take-or-pay debt-backed, some is collateralized by circular vendor financing (NVIDIA) rather than independent end-customer revenue. Diligence on the ultimate revenue source behind a lease is now a first-order underwriting question.
- The depreciation-schedule stretch (3-4yr → 5-6yr+) is a leading indicator to watch: if real-world GPU obsolescence (2-3yr cadence) forces write-downs or schedule reversals, hyperscaler earnings and thus capex appetite could compress quickly — a risk to future demand for new DC capacity that isn't visible in current MW/GW forecasts.
- The gap between "capital committed" (sticky, ~1% forecast movement) and "capacity delivered" (30-50% of 2026 plans slipping to 2028) means developers face a demand profile that is real in aggregate but poorly timed — power and interconnection queues, not compute demand, are the likely binding constraint through 2028.
- For compute buyers, circular financing arrangements (chip vendor as investor/landlord/lender) create correlated counterparty risk: a slowdown in one lab's spending could cascade through NVIDIA's guarantees to affect unrelated leases and neocloud balance sheets simultaneously.

## Open questions for deep dive
- What share of hyperscaler and neocloud 2026-2027 capex/backlog is financed or guaranteed, directly or indirectly, by NVIDIA (or another chip vendor), and how would a hypothetical NVIDIA guarantee call cascade through DC lease obligations?
- Given 5-6+ year depreciation schedules against 2-3 year real obsolescence, what is the plausible magnitude and timing of a hyperscaler earnings/capex correction, and how would that affect near-term (2027-2029) demand for new US data center leases?
- Which specific power markets/utilities have the worst interconnection queue backlogs driving the 30-50% capacity-slip estimate, and how does that map to where committed AI lab GW is concentrated (Texas, Virginia, etc.)?
